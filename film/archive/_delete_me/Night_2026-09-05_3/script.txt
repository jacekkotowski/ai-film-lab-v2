Seventy-two hours before D-Day, Eisenhower had two analysts telling him two different things.

One used twenty years of historical averages. June weather in Normandy was reliably calm — the pattern said go.

The other threw out the averages. He was pulling fresh readings from weather balloons and ships and stations from Spain to Iceland, building a real-time picture instead of a historical one. His model said a storm was forming, and the invasion had to wait.

That's the actual conflict in the film "Pressure" — not weather versus war, but two ways of doing analysis, and a decision-maker who has to pick one under a deadline he can't move.

Every data team has lived some version of this. The historical model is comfortable. The real-time signal is inconvenient. And the person who has to act on it isn't the one who built either model.

Eisenhower picked the harder read. It was the right call.
