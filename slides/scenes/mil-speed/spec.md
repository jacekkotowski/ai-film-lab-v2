# mil-speed — slide 5 of 8 of the mil-measure film

**Problem (Jacek's plan, 1:25–1:45):** a sport target car on a rope crosses
the view; its size (2 m) and distance are known; its position at 0.0, 0.5,
1.0 s measured in MIL; angular movement / time = angular speed, and with the
distance, linear speed.

**One idea:** mils per second on the reticle, times the distance, give the
target's speed.

**Data (chosen numbers, not a measured target)**
- Car 2 m long at 200 m: 2 × 1000 ÷ 200 = **10 mil** long (drawn to that).
- Its nose at 0, 5, 10 mil at 0.0, 0.5, 1.0 s → (10 − 0) ÷ 1.0 = **10 mil/s**.
- Speed = mil/s × distance ÷ 1000 = 10 × 200 ÷ 1000 = **2 m/s** = 2 × 3.6 =
  **7.2 km/h**.
- On the Aurora: 5 mil is the heavy dot, 10 mil the stadia marked "4";
  shown is the horizontal line from −10.6 to 10.6 mil at 0.37 units/mil,
  the stadia cut to ±0.7 mil.
- The reticle is held still (bipod); the car moves across it. **No lead**:
  a lead needs the time of flight, and we have none measured
  (`shooting-optics` §7).
