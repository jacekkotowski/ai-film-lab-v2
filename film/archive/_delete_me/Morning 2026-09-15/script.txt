I keep having the same dream, in different clothes.

In one, I'm back in college.
I got there early — pranked my way in a year ahead,
passed the exam before anyone expected me to. I wanted to prove to the girl I loved that I was worthy. And it didn't matter. Nobody asks for that anymore. I still don't belong. I lose my backpack — everything I own is in it, it's the only home I have. Inside, the last things from my childhood. And while I search, she watches and laughs.

In another, I'm leaving the country. Poland, empty, reforested, no work left. Five thousand zloty for a plane ticket out. I worry about what I'm leaving behind — my radios, my gadgets, the small                        useless things that were mine. I end up on a construction site holding a shovel, not working, just standing — because sitting means you've failed.

And underneath both of those is the one that isn't a dream. My daughter, dying in my hands. And after — being accused. Blamed for a crash I didn't cause.

Three versions of the same loss. Something that should hold steady, gone. And me, left standing there — holding nothing, or holding blame instead.

That's the film that keeps playing.
