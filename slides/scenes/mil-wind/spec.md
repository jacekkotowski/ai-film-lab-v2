# mil-wind — slide 7 of 8 of the mil-measure film

**Problem (Jacek's plan, 2:05–2:20):** bullet flies, wind blows from the
side, the path shifts; "wind drift increases with time of flight"; with
1 MIL = 10 cm @ 100 m the drift can be expressed in MIL.

**One idea:** wind drift comes from the air's delay (the lag), and a drift
in cm at a known distance is a hold in mil.

**Data**
- **Correction to the plan's reason.** "The longer it flies, the longer the
  wind pushes" is the popular explanation, but without air there would be no
  drift at all, however long the flight. The standard result (Didion's lag
  rule, exact for a flat-fire point mass): **drift = crosswind × (t − D/v₀)**,
  t = real time of flight, D = distance, v₀ = muzzle velocity; t − D/v₀ is
  the lag, how much longer the flight takes than it would in a vacuum.
  Same direction as the plan in practice: longer, slower flight → more lag →
  more drift.
- No example drift is computed from a bullet: we have no measured time of
  flight (`shooting-optics` §7).
- The conversion is geometry, exact: mil = cm × 10 ÷ m = 30 × 10 ÷ 300 =
  **1.0 mil**. 30 cm at 300 m is a plausible drift (chosen, not computed).
- Held into the wind: the impact moves downwind, so aim 1 mil upwind.
- The drawing: from above, the drift drawn as distance² and far wider than
  30 cm in 300 m. Shape only.
