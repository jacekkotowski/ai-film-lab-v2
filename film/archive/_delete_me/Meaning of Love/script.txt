My parents never used the word.

Love.

They were ashamed of it.

Hate words were easier.

One was home.
The other was in the cellar.

I was running up and down the stairs,
negotiating peace.

My father told me,

“Say sorry even if you were right.”

This was not love.

People are ashamed of tenderness.

I want to love.

I love you.

The heart races.

It is a risk.

Once, she knew I was going to say it.

“Forget me,” she said.
“It will be easier.”

For three months she had been weak.

Tuberculosis suspected.

Tall and hunched.

A bent birch tree.

Coughing.

I brought her tangerines.

And classes, literature on tapes.

We listened.

I stayed  hours when she was sick.

January.

February.

March.

I thought April was perfect.

Cherry and apple trees blooming.

Petals in the air.

I love you.

I didn't say it.

I was there.

Perhaps that is love.

Until she did not need me.

Again, my father told me.

You are a boy, boys do not complain.

I studied, noone believed in me

That I would pass entrance exams.

Only to prove I deserve.

I thought this was love.

Then I decided, love does not exist.

Yet.

I think I saw it once.

We were doing Combat Medical Training.

The casualty was unconscious.

One medic was doing everything correctly.

Airway.

Bleeding.

Move him.

The casualty's head bumped against the stretcher.

He didn't notice.

The other one did.

Warm hands on his cheeks.

Brushing the dirt away.

One hand gently under his head.

“Easy…”

“I've got you.”

“Stay with me.”

The casualty couldn't answer.

But he kept talking to him.

The commander noticed.

One man saw a casualty.

The other saw a human being.

And I think…

that's love.
