# face-implied — slide 4 of love-as-a-face-threatening-act

**Problem (Jacek's words):** Where does the threat live? Grice showed that
we mean more than we say. Words carry implicatures. Sperber and Wilson
went further: every act of communication arrives with a promise that it is
worth the hearer’s effort. So “I love you” makes her search. What does he
want? The threat lives in that search. In the implied request. It does not
live in the love.

**One idea:** the threat is not in what is said ("I love you") but in what
she searches for under it: the implied request, the same "?" weight as on
the slide before.

**Data** (no numbers: nothing here is measured)
- Two layers split by one line: "said" above (the words), "meant" below
  (Grice: we mean more than we say).
- The speaker, the hearer and "I love you" stand where they stood on
  face-ledger (x ±2.4, feet 2.6, words on baseline 4.9), so the two slides
  read as one scene.
- The weight is face-ledger's: SICK, "?" in the background colour. Here it
  is larger (1.6 × 1.4, "?" at 120) because it is the subject.
- Speaker ACCENT, hearer SECOND, the request SICK, the layer names DIM.

**Steps (one per sentence he says)**
