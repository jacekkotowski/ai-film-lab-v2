# zero-clicks — slide 2 of 4 of the zeroing film

**Problem (Jacek's script, section 2 "Turn centimetres into clicks"):**
the group sits 5 cm low and 4.5 cm left at 100 m (slide 1, `zero-group`).
How many clicks on each optic?

**One idea:** clicks = distance ÷ what one click moves. The same 5 cm is
cut into few big Triumph clicks or many small SLx clicks.

**Data** (100 m; 1 MOA = 100 m × tan(1/60°) = 2.909 cm)
| optic | click | cm per click | up (5 cm) | right (4.5 cm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vortex Triumph | ½ MOA | 1.454 → "1.45" | 5 / 1.454 = 3.44 → **3** | 4.5 / 1.454 = 3.09 → **3** |
| PA SLx 5x MicroPrism | ¼ MOA | 0.727 → "0.73" | 5 / 0.727 = 6.88 → **7** | 4.5 / 0.727 = 6.19 → **6** |

Drawn: the 5 cm as a bar, a tick at every click. Triumph: 3 ticks, last at
4.36 cm (short of 5). SLx: 7 ticks, last at 5.09 cm (just past 5). The
bar shows why the counts are rounded.

**Steps (one per sentence he says)**
1. line 0 — "clicks = cm ÷ click"
2. line 1 — the two optics, their click size, the 5 cm cut into clicks
3. line 2 — the counts: up 3 / right 3, and up 7 / right 6

**Narration (his script, section 2, with the 4.5 cm change)**
"Divide each distance by what one click moves. At 100 metres the
Triumph's half-MOA click moves the impact 1.45 centimetres, and the
SLx's quarter-MOA click moves it 0.73. A group five centimetres low and
four and a half to the left needs about three clicks each way on the
Triumph, but seven and six on the SLx."
