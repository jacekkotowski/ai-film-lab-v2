# mil-drop — slide 6 of 8 of the mil-measure film

**Problem (Jacek's plan, 1:45–2:05):** no table for one cartridge; the
principle: gravity acts continuously, drop ≈ ½gt²; time of flight ↑ → drop ↑;
faster bullet → shorter flight → less drop; the real path also depends on
BC, velocity, temperature, pressure.

**One idea:** the drop grows with the square of the time in the air.

**Data**
- drop = ½ g t², g = 9.81 m/s²: t = 0.5 s → ½ × 9.81 × 0.25 = **1.23 m**
  (on screen "1.2 m"); t = 1.0 s → **4.91 m** ("4.9 m"). Twice the time,
  four times the drop. The times are chosen; no cartridge is named.
- **It is the drop below the bore line** (the line the barrel points along).
  With air drag it still holds well for flat fire if t is the real time of
  flight: drag slows the bullet along its path and gravity pulls down
  whatever its speed. **The hold on the reticle is smaller**: the sight is
  zeroed, so the barrel already points up a little. Not said on the slide;
  the narration says "below the line of the barrel".
- The drawing: x ∝ t (the bullet's slowing is not drawn), y ∝ t², depth
  3.4 units for 4.9 m. The shape is the parabola; the depth is not to scale.
